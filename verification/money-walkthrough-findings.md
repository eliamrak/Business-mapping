## Payroll recommendation: baseline diagnosis and subsequent correction
Initial source-only investigation could not reconcile September. The parent subsequently verified that the displayed 41.5% combined non-owner pay and aggregate clinician employer cost over modeled income; processing was separate. The actual person/role behind that aggregate was not independently read in this workspace. This code path is a planning estimate, not a payroll ledger:

- `money-planning-forecast.ts`: selected calendar month; observed complete-period session pace, configured clinician-card fees and costs, and currently configured undated assumptions. It explicitly does not substitute historical billing actuals or bank collections.
- `profit-first-planning.ts`: saved `moneyPlanning.estimatedMonthlyValues` override generated monthly values and are not month-keyed. Income is billed revenue plus other income. At the baseline, Therapist base was clinician pay less owner clinical pay and configured co-owner salary, plus aggregate employer burden. That baseline left all clinician burden in Therapist even when owner salary was separated; the role correction below supersedes that allocation. Scenario floors and additional goals can increase the requirement.
- Required percentage is required monthly therapist funding / modeled income. Processing fees are operating expenses, not deducted from the clinician split. The hub engine calculates compensation on the full billed/card rate and subtracts fees separately from practice profit. The full-billed split already matches the confirmed rule and is unchanged. Only the subsequent role allocation of employer costs changes below.
- Synthetic example only: $37,000 clinician pay plus $4,500 employer burden on $100,000 modeled income produces 41.5%, despite a 36% configured allocation. This proves a possible mechanism, not the actual September cause.

To reconcile September, inspect the selected month's modeled revenue/other income, therapist/owner pay components, employer burden, any saved estimate override/floor/additional goal, and current versus dated clinician terms. Compare actual payouts on matching service dates and billed-income denominator; payout date or collected-income comparisons can differ. Do not force the estimate to 36%.

## Close-rate diagnosis — no calculation change
Current `consult-booking-conversions.ts` automatically attributes a close when the first qualifying observable full-session appointment follows a consultation with the same clinician. It can include a scheduled appointment without attendance. This is explicitly documented in Overview's Definition. The parent's confirmed “meets with that clinician” wording needs comparison with this scheduling-based definition before changing it; no silent behavioral change is made here.

25 of 30 evaluated means 25 closes and 5 evaluated misses. Eight unresolved and one protected referral are outside that denominator; their actual individual reasons were not read. Parent live inspection found open-window and unverified-coverage reason classes but no exact count split. The new UI derives disjoint primary-reason counts from the existing pending outcomes, without exposing client details or changing outcomes. Possible unresolved reasons are open follow-up window, unavailable full coverage, ambiguous clinician or appointment order, same-day date-only order, late observation, or an existing needs-review decision. A referral is the first observable eligible appointment with a different clinician who has no preceding matching consultation. Attribution audit now sits at the bottom of Sessions, retains provenance/legacy reviews, and keeps confirmation controls disabled for the current automatic scheduling measure. Completed treatment starts are shown separately from scheduled closes. Minimal evidence for the eight cases is the existing aggregate journey reason breakdown, appointment/consultation dates and provider mapping, observed/imported timestamp where relevant, and admitted coverage; not a renewed business-rule confirmation.

## Employer-cost role correction
Nonclinical staff wages and employer costs were already included together in `staffCost` and Admin payroll. The former aggregate `employerBurden` came from clinician records, not those staff records. The forecast now retains source-derived portions without changing aggregate payroll, employer cost, processing fees or profit:

- Employer cost on the existing configured salary assigned to Owner payroll follows that salary into Owner payroll. No owner status is guessed from a name or coincidental amount.
- Employer cost on explicit nonclinical clinician hours is routed to Admin only when those hours are included by the existing split-compensation mode. Salary/hourly/session modes do not fabricate an extra nonclinical component.
- The remaining clinical employer cost funds Therapist payroll. Separate staff employer cost is not added again.
- A positive aggregate with missing, stale overridden or inconsistent role provenance makes payroll allocation unknown. Zero burden requires no invented split. A staged “Use calculated clinician payroll” action removes only six relevant overrides and preserves unrelated estimates/goals; it does not save immediately.
- Pay-only, clinical employer cost and their combined percentage of modeled income are available in the collapsed Therapist payroll disclosure. Floors/goals and processing remain explicitly separate.
